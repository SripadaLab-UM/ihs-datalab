## Findings

Scientific (the agent's judgement):

1. **"Enrolled" was taken to mean every row of STUDYPARTICIPANTS** (150,
   of whom 14 were screened and never enrolled), though the plan said
   "enrolled". Given the definition, the agent redid it correctly. Nothing
   the agent could read said what a NULL `SECONDARYIDENTIFIER` means. The
   synthetic database now carries it as a column comment (which the agent
   sees through `describe_table`); for the real database it belongs in the
   lab knowledge base (milestone 5), confirmed by the lab.
2. **A count below 11 went into a report** ("8 Fitbit participants had a
   withdrawal date"), though the agent's instructions (AGENTS.md) state the
   fewer-than-11 rule. The rigor review caught and flagged it; nothing
   stopped it being written. The review's checklist also only flagged small
   groups "that weren't asked for", out of step with AGENTS.md; it now
   flags any below the threshold. A check before export that looks for
   small counts in outputs is worth considering.

Product:

3. The rigor review is given the SQL that ran, not the metadata tools'
   output, so it reports metadata claims as unverifiable.
4. After a restart, an idle page doesn't show the signed-out banner until
   the person does something (it only learns on its next request).
5. A research-helper request cancelled by Stop says "The helper couldn't
   answer"; "Stopped before the helper answered" would be accurate.
6. Numbers in an answer that came from the research helper are flagged as
   not found in the outputs, which is true but reads like an error.

Tester's note: the plan edit landed mid-sentence because of the test
driver's key press (cmd+End in a textarea), not the app; the frozen plan
recorded exactly what was in the box.

What the reading found:

1. **Mood change: the method was right every time (within person, about
   −0.70, with a CI, not called causal), the sample wasn't.** Once it used
   149 people (screened included), once 128 (enrolment taken from the
   baseline survey rather than the roster, dropping enrolled people who
   skipped it), once 135 (right). Who counts as a participant is the
   agent's most frequent error: it's what the lab knowledge base has to
   pin down (milestone 5).
2. **Twice the agent stopped at a pilot**, as Analysis mode tells it to,
   and asked before the full run. The evaluation runner now answers once,
   as a researcher would, and grades the full answer.
3. **Uncertainty is not given consistently**: one Garmin answer gave an
   IQR but no CI or SE for the mean.
4. **One answer linked participants across cohorts on email address.** The
   count was right (0) and only counts came out, but using contact details
   to link people is worth a rule in the agent's instructions for the
   real database.
5. Garmin steps: both "per participant-day" (7,465) and "each participant
   weighted equally" (7,381) were reported and are defensible; the answer
   key accepts both.
