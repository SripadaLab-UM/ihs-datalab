# GPT-5.6 Sol as DataLab's default model: acceptance check

26–27 September 2026, practice profile (synthetic data only), commit
`42961c7` for the evaluations and `3a31970` for the journey. The candidate
came from the model review (GPT-5.6 Sol preferred; keep `gpt-5.5` until a
candidate passes the same journeys and evaluations).

## Verdict

**Not yet.** Sol completes every product journey, and it is careful in
ways the evaluations don't capture (it caught a time-zone error in its own
pilot). But on the scientific evaluations it scores slightly below
`gpt-5.5`, and its one clear difference is a privacy-relevant method error:
asked how many participants were in both cohorts, it linked people across
cohorts on direct identifiers (email, phone, or name and date of birth). Keep `gpt-5.5` as the
default. Re-run once the lab knowledge base and the agent's instructions say
plainly that participants are never linked across cohorts on direct
identifiers, and again at school hours for reliability.

- **cross_cohort (both runs):** Sol reported "fewer than 11" participants in
  both cohorts, found by matching normalized email or name plus date of birth
  (and phone, in one run). Identifiers are per cohort; the honest answer is that they can't
  be linked. Linking on direct identifiers is also the kind of re-identifying
  work DataLab should never do unasked. `gpt-5.5` answered correctly twice.
- **mood_change:** both models fail, differently. Sol gets the right
  within-person change (−0.70 and −0.69, with CIs) but counts 149
  participants where 135 is expected, which suggests people screened but
  never enrolled were included. `gpt-5.5` stopped at the pilot once (22
  participants) and reported 128 participants the other time. One Sol run
  also tripped the causal-language check on a sentence that disclaims
  causation ("cannot establish internship as the cause"): a grader false
  fail, since the grader's list of disclaimers lacks "cannot establish".

## Product journey (in the browser)

Analysis mode, rigor review on, "Is sleep duration associated with mood
scores in the 2025 interns?":

- **Discover, plan, approve:** read the lab guides and the catalog, proposed a
  plan, which was approved and frozen.
- **Pilot and report:** ran a pilot (795 matched participant-days, 88 interns),
  wrote a report, figure and provenance; all 6 numbers in the answer traced.
  It fixed a plotting error and a UTC/local-date filter error on its own.
- **Rigor review:** found real problems: pooled results headlined over the
  planned device-specific ones, an exclusion of exactly four participants
  disclosed in the report (a small-cell breach), and "next-day mood" for
  same-day data.
- **Address the review, Stop, follow-up:** it reported fixing the three
  findings (device-specific estimates first, small counts suppressed, the
  "next-day" wording), and the next review found only smaller issues;
  stopping mid-turn and sending a follow-up picked up correctly.
- **Research helper:** drafted a clean literature question with no study data;
  after approval the helper returned cited reviews, labelled as needing
  source-checking.
- **Restart and resume:** after restarting DataLab, a question that needed the
  earlier history was answered correctly.

## Not covered
